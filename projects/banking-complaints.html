Consumer Banking Complaints Analysis

Python • MySQL • Tableau

An end-to-end analysis of U.S. consumer banking complaints from 2021 through 2025, examining complaint trends, recurring issues, company responses, resolution outcomes, and geographic patterns.

Project Overview

This end-to-end project analyzes U.S. consumer complaints submitted to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau from 2021 through 2025. It was developed to help compliance and customer-experience teams identify growing complaint areas, recurring consumer issues, companies requiring closer review, and differences in complaint-resolution outcomes.

The project covers the complete analytical workflow, from API extraction and database preparation to SQL analysis and interactive Tableau dashboards.

Data Source

The project uses public data from the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's Consumer Complaint Database. The analysis covers complaints received from 2021 through 2025 across five product groups: cards, checking and savings accounts, money transfers and digital payments, mortgages and vehicle loans and leases.

Data Preparation and Workflow

Complaint records were extracted from the CFPB API using Python and imported into MySQL. The database was then cleaned and prepared by standardizing fields, reviewing missing values, removing duplicate records, and creating indexes to support more efficient analysis.

SQL was used to examine changes in complaint volume, product and issue distributions, company-level patterns, response outcomes, and geographic concentrations. The resulting data was then prepared for visualization in Tableau.

Analysis and Key Findings

Consumer complaint volume rose substantially from 2021 to 2025. Cards and checking and savings accounts generated the largest complaint volumes, while complaints involving digital payments grew especially quickly. Since most companies responded on time, response timeliness alone provided limited insight into performance.

Compliance and customer-experience teams should prioritize companies and products with high complaint volumes, investigate frequently recurring issues, and compare whether complaints were resolved through explanations, monetary relief, or non-monetary relief. Because complaint totals are not adjusted for company size or market share, they should be treated as signals for further investigation rather than direct company-performance rankings.
